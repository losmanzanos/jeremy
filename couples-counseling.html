The Work

The pattern is the client. Not either of you.

Couples counseling for two people who can predict the whole argument and have it anyway. Telehealth across Colorado.

You can both predict the entire argument. You know your line, you know the response, you know the point about forty seconds in where it stops being about the dishes. And you have it anyway, on a Tuesday, for the hundredth time.

The pattern is the client here. Not you, and not your partner. That is not a diplomatic way of avoiding the question of who is right. It is the actual working assumption, because in almost every couple I see, two reasonable people are running a loop that neither of them designed and both of them maintain.

What I do in the room

I interrupt. Frequently. Couples work where the therapist lets the argument run its usual course and then comments on it afterward is comfortable and mostly useless, because you already know how that argument goes.

So we stop it in the middle. What did you just hear. What did you mean. What happened in your body at the point where your voice changed. It is slower and more irritating than arguing, and it is where the pattern actually becomes visible.

What I do not do

  • Take sides, or arbitrate who was factually correct about an evening in 2019
  • Keep secrets between you, which means what one of you tells me individually is not sealed off from the work
  • Assume the goal is staying together

That last one matters. Sometimes the useful outcome is a clearer, kinder decision to separate, particularly where there are children and the relationship is going to continue in some form regardless. I will not steer you toward that. I also will not pretend it is off the table.

When one of you is not sure about therapy

Extremely common, and not a reason to wait. Come anyway. The skeptical partner is often the more useful one in the room, because they will say the true thing early instead of performing cooperation for six weeks.

Where couples work is not the right call

If there is violence, coercion, or fear in the relationship, joint sessions can make things less safe rather than more. That work needs to happen individually first. If that describes your situation, tell me on the consultation call and we will talk about the right sequence.
