The Work

The hardest part often starts after you leave.

Therapy for survivors of domestic violence and coercive control, at a pace you set. Telehealth across Colorado.

Leaving is treated as the end of the story. It is closer to the middle. The danger changes shape, the adrenaline that kept you sharp for years has nowhere to go, and you are supposed to feel relieved on a schedule that other people find reasonable.

What usually shows up instead is a body that has not been told it is over. You check the locks more than once. A car that slows near the house means something. Someone raises their voice three tables away in a restaurant and you have already mapped the exits before you notice you are doing it.

What a year later actually looks like

People come in embarrassed about the size of the things that still affect them. A tone of voice. A specific hour of the evening. The sound of keys. They expect me to tell them that is disproportionate.

It is not disproportionate. It is a set of predictions your nervous system built under conditions where being wrong was expensive, and it is going to take more than a change of address to update them. The work is updating them, deliberately, at a pace you set.

The part people rarely say out loud

Often there is something you miss. A version of them, an early period, the way it felt when things were good. That does not make you confused or complicit, and it is not evidence that you should have stayed. It is one of the ordinary, awful facts of having loved someone who also harmed you, and it is welcome in here.

Where we start

We start with what actually happened, in whatever order you can tell it, and we stop when you want to stop. I do not push for detail I do not need. There is a version of trauma work that treats a full chronological account as the price of admission, and I do not practice it.

  • What is happening now, this week, in your body and your day
  • Safety and logistics, if any part of that is still unresolved
  • The specific triggers that are costing you the most, one at a time
  • Memory work, including EMDR, only when and if you want it

You decide the pace and you can change it any week. That is not a courtesy. In work like this the pacing is the treatment, because the experience of being the one who decides is most of what was taken.

If you have not left

You are still welcome here. I am not going to make your care conditional on a decision you have not made, and I am not going to spend our hour building a case. People leave when leaving becomes possible, and possible is usually made of small practical things stacked up over months.

If you are in immediate danger or thinking about harming yourself, call or text 988, or call 911. The Colorado Crisis Line is 1-844-493-8255, or text TALK to 38255.
